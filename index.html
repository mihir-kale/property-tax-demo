    About this comparison

    This comparison holds the property’s value constant and changes only the county tax rate. The benchmark models an inflation-plus-population limit: HB 1089 requires county levy limits but does not itself specify this formula. [source]

    It is a look at the current county rate relative to a 2016-to-2025 inflation-plus-population scenario — not a forecast of an enacted future rate, and it does not model changes in services, other taxes/fees, or economic responses.

    How this is calculated

    Actual county rate: per $100 (FY2025–26 official Mecklenburg county rate).

    Scenario rate: derived by capping the inflation-plus-population benchmark levy at the actual levy and dividing by the total taxable base.

    Actual county levy: (FY2025–26).

    County taxable base: (FY2025–26 assessed valuation).

    2016 baseline levy: (FY2016–17).

    2025 benchmark ceiling: — compounding the 2016 baseline through inflation+population transitions.

    Scenario levy (capped): = min(actual, ceiling).

    Scenario rate: 100 × ÷ = per $100.

    Rate reconciliation: between the reported levy and rate × base ÷ 100.

    Then for a property: bill = value × rate ÷ 100; difference = actual bill − benchmark bill.

    Inflation is the U.S. CPI-U annual average (BLS). Population is Mecklenburg annual resident estimates (U.S. Census). The transition into year T uses that year’s inflation and population change.

    Assumptions: the ceiling compounds from the 2016 baseline; a year below the ceiling does not ratchet it down; no separate new-construction allowance or overrides. The scenario never prescribes an increase — if the actual levy is already below the ceiling, the actual rate is retained.
